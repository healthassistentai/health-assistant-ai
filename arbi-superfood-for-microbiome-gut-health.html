Arbi: The Underrated Gut-Friendly Food

Arbi, also known as taro root, is a starchy vegetable containing dietary fiber and resistant starch.

Why Resistant Starch Matters

Resistant starch can reach the colon without being fully digested in the small intestine.
Reaches the large intestine
Feeds gut microorganisms
Can be fermented into short-chain fatty acids
May support a healthy gut environment

Research on taro has specifically investigated its resistant starch, fiber and prebiotic potential.

Arbi & Your Gut Microbiome

Dietary fiber supports regular bowel movements

Fermentation produces short-chain fatty acids

Always Cook Arbi Thoroughly

Never eat raw or undercooked arbi
Cook thoroughly before eating
Start with a small portion
Drink enough water
Avoid eating very large portions at once

Proper cooking is important because raw or undercooked taro can contain calcium oxalate crystals that may cause irritation.
